Reading the Transformer paper, slowly

TL;DR
A one-paragraph summary of the paper and why it matters. State the core contribution, the result that surprised you, and who should care. [ this whole article is a reusable template — replace the prose, keep the structure ]

Open with the problem the paper is solving and the context a reader needs. Two or three sentences of setup is usually enough — assume the reader is technical but new to this specific subfield. Link out to prerequisites rather than re-explaining them.

Then state, in your own words, what the authors actually did. Resist copying the abstract; the value you add is the plain-language translation.

The key idea

Walk through the central mechanism. This is the heart of the post — go slow. Use an analogy if it helps, but always tie it back to the concrete operation. When a method has a name, define it once and reuse it.

When there's a clean equation worth highlighting, set it apart so it can breathe:

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( Q·Kᵀ / √dk ) · V

Why it works

Give the intuition behind the result. A good test: could the reader now explain this to a colleague at lunch? If not, you've skipped a step.

"Drop in a memorable quote from the paper, or a sentence of your own that crystallizes the takeaway."

When code makes it concrete, keep it minimal — the smallest snippet that conveys the idea:

attention.py 
# scaled dot-product attention
def attention(q, k, v):
    scores = (q @ k.transpose(-2, -1)) / math.sqrt(q.size(-1))
    weights = scores.softmax(dim=-1)
    return weights @ v

Limitations & open questions

Be honest about what the paper doesn't solve. A few bullets work well here:

  • The assumption that quietly does a lot of work.
  • Where the method is likely to break in practice.
  • The experiment you wish they'd run.
Key takeaways
  1. The single most important thing to remember.
  2. The second-order insight worth keeping.
  3. What you'd try next if you were extending this.
